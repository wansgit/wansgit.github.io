How to Convert RAW Files to JPG: 5 Best Methods

August 20, 2026 7 min read Adi Pratama
Tutorial

RAW format stores all the data captured by the sensor, providing maximum flexibility in post-processing, but resulting in very large file sizes that can't be shared directly like regular JPG photos. I first experienced this problem when I bought my first DSLR camera. I was thrilled because the photos I took looked amazing. But when I tried to upload them to Instagram? Failed. RAW files weren't recognized at all. They couldn't even be opened on my home computer because I hadn't installed the right software. That's when I realized that RAW files need to be converted before they can be used.

The problem is, RAW files can't be uploaded directly to social media, sent via email, or viewed on most devices without specialized software. That's why converting RAW to JPG is one of the most common tasks every photographer has to perform. In this guide, we'll discuss the five best methods for converting RAW files to JPG, ranging from free software to professional tools, so you can choose the method that best suits your needs and budget.

What Is the RAW Format?

Before diving into conversion methods, it's important to understand what the RAW format actually is. The RAW format is a raw image format that stores camera sensor data completely without in-camera processing. Unlike JPG, which is compressed and processed by the camera's processor, RAW stores all the light, color, and detail information captured by the sensor.

Every camera brand has a different RAW format. Canon uses .CR2 or .CR3, Nikon uses .NEF, Sony uses .ARW, FujiFilm uses .RAF, and Panasonic uses .RW2. Additionally, there are also universal RAW formats like .DNG (Digital Negative) developed by Adobe to be compatible with various camera brands.

The main advantage of the RAW format is its flexibility in post-processing. Because RAW stores all the raw data from the sensor, you can adjust exposure, white balance, contrast, and color with much better quality than doing the same on a JPG file. This is why many professional photographers prefer to shoot in RAW format.

Why Do RAW Files Need to Be Converted to JPG?

Although RAW is superior in terms of quality and flexibility, there are many reasons why RAW files need to be converted to JPG:

  • Compatibility: RAW files can only be opened by specialized software like Adobe Lightroom, Capture One, or the RAW reader from each camera brand. JPG can be opened on virtually every device and application without issues.
  • File size: RAW files can be 20-50MB per photo, while JPG is usually only 2-8MB depending on resolution and compression quality. This has a significant impact on storage and file transfer.
  • Upload speed: Uploading RAW files to social media or websites is very slow due to their large size. JPG can be uploaded quickly because of its much smaller size.
  • Printing: For most printing needs, JPG quality is already more than sufficient. Larger RAW files don't always produce better prints.
  • Sharing final results: After editing a RAW file, the final result is usually exported in JPG format to share with clients or the public.

Method 1: Adobe Lightroom (Professional)

Adobe Lightroom is one of the most popular photo editing software programs among professional photographers and serious hobbyists. Lightroom can not only convert RAW to JPG, but also gives you full control over the editing process before conversion.

Here are the steps to convert RAW to JPG in Adobe Lightroom:

  1. Import RAW files: Open Lightroom and click the Import button. Select the RAW files you want to convert. Lightroom supports almost all RAW formats from various camera brands.
  2. Edit photos: Before exporting, make any necessary edits such as exposure adjustment, white balance, contrast, color, and cropping. This is Lightroom's main advantage -- you can get the best results from RAW files before converting them.
  3. Click Export: After you're satisfied with the edits, click the Export button in the upper left corner. An export settings window will appear.
  4. Choose JPG format: In the Export Settings section, select JPEG as the file format. Set the JPEG quality (70-85% is already excellent for most needs), resolution, and image size according to your needs.
  5. Click Export: Choose the save location for the converted files and click Export. The conversion process will run based on the number of photos being exported.

Lightroom also supports batch export, meaning you can convert hundreds of RAW photos to JPG at once with consistent settings. This feature is extremely useful for photographers who have many photos to process after a shoot.

My experience: Lightroom is indeed paid software, but this investment is absolutely worth it if you're serious about photography. I personally started with the 7-day trial version, and I've never stopped subscribing since. The preset feature alone has saved me hours every week.

Method 2: GIMP (Free)

GIMP (GNU Image Manipulation Program) is a free and open-source image editing software that can be an excellent alternative to Photoshop. GIMP supports opening and editing RAW files using the Darktable or RawTherapee plugin as a RAW processing backend.

Steps to convert RAW to JPG in GIMP:

  1. Install GIMP and the appropriate RAW plugin (Darktable or RawTherapee) on your computer.
  2. Open GIMP, click File > Open, and select the RAW file you want to convert.
  3. The Darktable or RawTherapee window will open automatically to process the RAW file. Make any necessary adjustments like exposure, white balance, and color grading.
  4. When finished, click Export to send the results to GIMP.
  5. In GIMP, make additional edits if needed, then click File > Export As, select JPEG format, set the quality, and save.

GIMP is an excellent choice for users looking for a free yet powerful solution for converting and editing RAW photos. Although not as feature-rich as Lightroom, GIMP offers very comprehensive editing features for a free software.

Method 3: RawTherapee (Free, Open Source)

RawTherapee is a free RAW editing software specifically designed for processing RAW files. This software supports almost all RAW formats from various camera brands and offers very detailed control over the RAW development process.

RawTherapee's advantage is its interface, which is specifically designed for RAW work, making it more intuitive for photographers who are familiar with RAW processing workflows. This software also has debugging tools that help users understand how each adjustment affects the image.

To convert RAW to JPG in RawTherapee:

  1. Open RawTherapee and navigate to the folder containing your RAW files
  2. Select the RAW file you want to convert and double-click to open it in the Editor
  3. Make any necessary adjustments using the tool panels on the right side
  4. When satisfied, click the Export button at the bottom of the interface
  5. Select JPEG format, set the output quality and size, then click Export

RawTherapee also supports batch processing, so you can convert many RAW files to JPG at once with the same parameters. This saves a tremendous amount of time for photographers who have hundreds of RAW photos to process.

Method 4: Online Converters (Fast)

If you don't want to install additional software on your computer, there are several online services that can convert RAW to JPG directly in the browser. Some of the best online services for RAW to JPG conversion:

  • CloudConvert: Supports many RAW formats and offers adjustable quality options. Free for a few conversions per day.
  • Convertio: A versatile online converter that supports almost all RAW formats from various camera brands.
  • Online-Convert: Offers RAW to JPG conversion with various quality and output size options.
  • Raw.pics.io: Specifically designed for RAW conversion, supports drag and drop and batch conversion.

The advantage of online converters is ease of access and no need to install software. However, there are some drawbacks to consider:

  • File size limitations: Many online services limit the file size that can be uploaded.
  • Internet requirement: You need a stable internet connection to upload and download files.
  • Privacy: Your photo files will be uploaded to third-party servers, which could be an issue for sensitive photos.
  • Quality: Some online services may not offer the same quality control as desktop software.

Method 5: Camera Manufacturer Software

Almost every camera brand includes built-in software that can convert RAW to JPG. This software is usually available for free with the camera and is specifically designed for RAW formats from that camera brand.

  • Canon Digital Photo Professional: Canon's software for converting and editing CR2/CR3 RAW files
  • Nikon Capture NX-D: Nikon's software for converting and editing NEF RAW files
  • Sony Imaging Edge: Sony's software for converting and editing ARW RAW files
  • FujiFilm X Raw Studio: FujiFilm's software for converting and editing RAF RAW files
  • Panasonic SILKYPIX: Panasonic's software for converting and editing RW2 RAW files

Camera manufacturer software usually offers the best conversion quality for RAW formats from their respective brands because it's developed by the camera manufacturer itself. However, this software typically only supports RAW formats from specific camera brands, so it's not suitable if you use cameras from multiple different brands.

Tip: If you use cameras from multiple different brands, consider using the DNG (Digital Negative) format from Adobe. DNG is a universal RAW format that can be opened by almost all photo editing software, avoiding compatibility issues between camera brands.

Conversion Method Comparison

To help you choose the most appropriate method, here's a brief comparison of the five methods above:

  • Adobe Lightroom: Best choice for professional photographers. Provides full control over editing and conversion, but requires a monthly subscription.
  • GIMP: A powerful free alternative. Suitable for users who want free software with comprehensive editing features.
  • RawTherapee: Best choice for users looking for free software specifically for RAW processing. Interface specifically designed for RAW work.
  • Online Converters: Fastest solution for occasional conversion. No software installation needed, but has file size and privacy limitations.
  • Camera Manufacturer Software: Best choice for users who use only one camera brand. Best quality for RAW formats from a specific brand.

Tips for Converting RAW to JPG

Here are some important tips for getting the best quality when converting RAW to JPG:

I learned some of these tips from my own mistakes, so hopefully they can help you avoid the same path. Most importantly: don't rush when exporting. Take the time to check the results before sending to a client or uploading to the web.

  1. Don't compress too low: Use a JPG quality of at least 80% to maintain image quality. Below 70%, compression artifacts start becoming clearly visible, especially in areas with smooth color gradients.
  2. Pay attention to color: Make sure white balance and color profiles are correct before exporting to JPG. JPG has lower color depth than RAW, so color adjustments after conversion will result in worse quality.
  3. Appropriate resolution: For the web, 72 dpi resolution with a width of 1920-2400 pixels is already more than sufficient. For printing, use 300 dpi resolution with sizes appropriate for the print job.
  4. Use sRGB color space: For the web and social media, use the sRGB color space because it's the standard for screen display. For professional printing, use Adobe RGB or ProPhoto RGB.
  5. Batch consistency: When converting many photos, use the same presets or settings for all photos so the results are consistent.

Conclusion

Converting RAW files to JPG is an important task for every photographer. By understanding the various conversion methods available, you can choose the solution that best suits your needs, skills, and budget. Whether you choose Adobe Lightroom for its professional features, GIMP or RawTherapee for a powerful free solution, online converters for ease of access, or camera manufacturer software for the best quality, the most important thing is that you get conversion results that meet your quality standards.

Remember that converting RAW to JPG is not just about changing file formats -- it's also the last opportunity to ensure that your photos have the best quality before sharing them. Take advantage of RAW editing flexibility before exporting to JPG to get the best results.
